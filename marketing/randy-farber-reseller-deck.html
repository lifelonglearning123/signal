Your main objection, answered

Your clients never touch GoHighLevel.

You said the more they can reach, the more they break, and the more they call you. So the client never gets the full thing. They get a stripped-back CRM with five areas in it.

What the client sees

  • Contacts — their own list, nothing else.
  • Conversation — calls, chats and messages in one thread.
  • Pipeline — where each job stands.
  • Calendar — bookings the AI made for them.
  • Social media planner — scheduled posts.

It works properly on a phone browser, which the standard app does not. Your tradespeople can check calls from a van.

What stays with you

  • The agent script. They can read every conversation but cannot change a word of how it answers.
  • Agent building. You can build every client’s receptionist from your own account and hand over access afterwards.
  • Automations, funnels and campaigns — you keep the full toolset behind the scenes.
  • Whether they get the builder at all. It can be switched off per client from your side.

The forwarding charge, explained

A caller asks for the owner. The AI says it will put them through and transfers the call. The caller’s line stays open the whole time, so telephony keeps charging for it. That onward cost is billed as a separate forwarding rate, set per minute, so a transferred call never quietly eats your margin. You choose whether to pass it on or absorb it — set it to zero and forwarded calls are free.

Give every agency its own keys

Each agency brings its own voice, telephony and CRM credentials. Two reasons, and the second is the one that matters at your scale: their spend lands on their own bill, and if an agency you have never met does something reckless with outbound calling, the liability is theirs and not yours. With a network of a hundred sub-agencies, that separation is the whole game.

Your Philippines plan

What you described is the thing this was built for.

People come in at no cost, sell under their own brand, and you take a share. Here is how each piece of your plan maps onto what already exists — and the three parts that still need working out.

Already works

  • They join at no upfront cost. You set what each agency pays, including nothing to start.
  • It looks like their own business. Own domain, logo, colours, prices. Nothing points back to you.
  • You build the agents. From your own account, then hand over access. They never need to learn the builder.
  • You see everything. A month-end report across every agency and every sub-account beneath them — including call transcripts, so you can check what is being said in your name.
  • They only have to market. Branded decks and videos are already generated for them on day one.

Still to work out — honestly

  • Collecting money in the Philippines. Each agency normally connects its own Stripe account. Where local banking makes that impractical, the alternative is running billing through your account and paying them out yourself. Worth a conversation before you commit.
  • Server cost per agent. Spinning up each new agent has a real cost. At a hundred-plus agencies we should agree how that is handled rather than discover it.
  • Outbound calling. Inbound receptionist is live today. Outbound is in development — don’t sell it yet.
